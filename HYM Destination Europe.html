Europe

The Depth Is What
Most People Miss.

Italy, France, Spain, Portugal, and Greece contain some of the most traveled destinations in the world and some of the most overlooked ones. The difference between a European trip that checks boxes and one that stays with you for twenty years is almost always the same thing: knowing which version of each place you're actually getting.

The Region

Europe Has a Tourist
Version and a Real One

Millions of people visit Florence every year. Almost none of them spend two mornings in the Uffizi with an art historian who limits the visit to a dozen paintings and explains what was at stake in each one. Almost none of them make it to Emilia-Romagna to stand in a Parmigiano cave at five in the morning or sit at a Tuesday lunch in Bologna with a family that has been making the same pasta for four generations. Those experiences don't show up on the highlights tour. They exist in the gap between a good European itinerary and an exceptional one.

The same logic applies across the continent. The Amalfi Coast has ten resorts within a half-mile of each other — one of them is worth booking and the others are expensive ways to be disappointed. Portugal's Alentejo region is one of the great under-traveled food and wine destinations in the world, forty-five minutes from Lisbon, almost entirely unknown to international tourists. The Basque Country sits between France and Spain and belongs to neither — a food culture that has no equivalent anywhere else, and a txoko dinner accessible only through the right connection.

We plan European travel around that gap. Not the highlights, but the version behind them — the properties that actually deliver, the guides who are genuine specialists, the access that requires knowing who to call.

When to Go

Europe by Season

Shoulder Season
April – June & September – October
The best European travel window by a significant margin. Crowds at major sites are manageable. Temperatures across the Mediterranean, Tuscany, and Provence are ideal. September and October add harvest season in Burgundy, Tuscany, and the Douro Valley — the single best window for food and wine travel across the continent. Rates at top properties are meaningfully lower than peak summer.
Best Overall Window
Summer
July – August
The Mediterranean, Greek islands, and Scandinavian coasts are at their best for sailing and long evenings. July and August in Italy, France, and Spain bring crowds at iconic sites and significantly elevated hotel rates. The Greek islands and Croatian coast perform well in summer for yacht charters and island-hopping. Scotland and Ireland are excellent in summer — warm, green, and considerably emptier than the south.
Winter
November – March
The Christmas markets of Germany, Austria, and Prague reward the cold. Paris in November is the best-kept secret in European travel — every restaurant has availability, every museum has room to breathe, and the city looks like itself. London in winter is exceptional for theatre. The Canary Islands and Madeira offer warm Atlantic island weather when mainland Europe closes for the season. Ski season in the Alps from December through March.

Common Questions

Europe — FAQ

Questions about timing, itinerary depth, country combinations, and how to get more out of European travel than most people do.

When is the best time to visit Europe?+
May–June is ideal for most of Western Europe — warm, less crowded than July–August, and attractions fully open. September–October delivers excellent weather, harvest seasons in wine country, and noticeably fewer tourists. July and August are the most popular but also the most crowded and expensive. Winter is excellent for cities — Paris, Rome, and Vienna are best without summer crowds.
How many countries can I realistically cover in two weeks?+
Two to three countries allows genuine depth. Four or more produces a rushed experience — too much transit, too little time in each place. There is a strong argument for spending two weeks in one country (Italy, France, Spain) rather than rushing through five. The best European trips go deeper rather than wider.
How do I balance city travel with the European countryside?+
City-country combinations produce the richest trips. Rome and Tuscany. London and the Cotswolds. Paris and Burgundy or Provence. Barcelona and the Costa Brava. A week in a city followed by several days in rural surroundings suits most travelers better than exclusively one or the other.
Which European destinations do you know most deeply?+
Italy (Rome, Florence, Tuscany, the Amalfi Coast, and the Dolomites), France (Paris, Provence, Burgundy, and the Riviera), Spain (Barcelona, Andalucía, and the Basque Country), Portugal (Lisbon and the Douro Valley), Greece (Athens, Santorini, and the Peloponnese), and the UK and Ireland. I visit these regions regularly and have vetted supplier relationships throughout.
Is a European river cruise worth considering?+
For the right traveler, yes. A Danube or Rhine cruise covers multiple countries efficiently with the simplicity of unpacking once. Shore time at each port is typically limited, so it suits travelers who want a comfortable overview rather than a deep dive. The better lines — Scenic, Uniworld, Avalon — deliver meaningfully higher quality than the mass-market alternatives.
Do I need visas for European travel?+
US and Canadian passport holders can visit Schengen Area countries for up to 90 days within any 180-day period without a visa. The EU's ETIAS system will eventually require pre-travel authorization. The UK has a separate Electronic Travel Authorisation since Brexit. Entry requirements for non-US/Canadian travelers vary — I verify current requirements for every client and passport combination.
What's the best way to see Italy for a first-time visitor?+
Rome (3 nights minimum) and Tuscany (3–4 nights, ideally based in a rural villa or Siena) cover the essentials. Adding Florence and the Amalfi Coast extends to an ideal 10–14 day itinerary. High-speed rail connects cities efficiently. A private guide in Rome transforms the experience — the Colosseum, the Forum, and the Vatican are exponentially better with the right context and access.
How far in advance should I book Europe travel?+
Summer peak season (July–August) requires 6–9 months for the best hotel inventory. Shoulder season travel (May–June, September–October) can be arranged in 3–4 months. Private villa rentals in Tuscany, Vatican early-access bookings, and certain Michelin-star reservations benefit from booking as far in advance as possible.
