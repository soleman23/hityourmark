Why Safari Travel

The Difference Is Almost
Entirely the Guide

A safari built around the right camp with the right guide is one of the most extraordinary experiences in travel. A safari built around the wrong ones is a long, dusty week of disappointment. The difference is almost never reflected in the brochure.

We've personally visited every camp we recommend — most of them more than once. We know which lodges treat their guides as employees and which treat them as partners. We know which conservancies enforce vehicle limits and which silently let safari trucks pile up around a leopard sighting. Those distinctions are the trip.

The questions we ask before placing you in a camp are not the questions most agents think to ask. How long has the head guide been there? What's the camp's policy on off-roading? How many vehicles do they put on a single sighting? Get those answers right, and the rest of the trip takes care of itself.

When to Go

Safari Season by Season

Dry Season
May – October
The benchmark window for southern and East Africa. Wildlife concentrates around water sources, vegetation thins, and visibility is exceptional.
Best Time to Safari
Shoulder
November & April
Quieter camps, lower rates, and excellent birding. The brief windows on either side of the rains. Often the most rewarding for repeat visitors.
Green Season
December – March
Calving season in the south. Lush landscapes, dramatic skies, fewer guests, and meaningfully lower rates. Best for photographers and birders.
