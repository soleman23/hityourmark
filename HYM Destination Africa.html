Every traveler who has been on a genuine African safari says the same thing: nothing they had read or seen prepared them for what it actually was. The scale of the landscape. The Region

The Camp Is Everything.
The Guide Is More.

There are over a thousand safari camps and lodges operating across sub-Saharan Africa. The range in quality between the best and the rest is one of the largest in any travel category anywhere in the world. A top-ten Botswana camp and a mid-tier Tanzania camp cost within a few hundred dollars of each other per night. The experience they deliver is not in the same category.

The guide is the variable that matters most. A guide who has worked the same ecosystem for fifteen years reads animal behavior in ways that a less experienced guide doesn't — predicting where the leopard will move before it moves, positioning the vehicle for the light before the light arrives, staying at a sighting for the right amount of time instead of moving on when the drama pauses. That difference is only available at camps that hire and retain the right people, which is a short list that takes years to develop.

Africa also has more to offer than the safari circuit. Morocco is one of the great cultural travel destinations in the world. South Africa combines world-class wine country, penguin colonies, the Cape Peninsula, and private game reserves with malaria-free Big Five access. Rwanda's gorilla treks are among the most profound wildlife experiences on earth. We know all of it — and we know which version of each destination is worth the flight.

Six African Destinations Worth Planning Properly

Each has a definitive window, a specific set of camps worth booking, and a version of the experience that most travelers never find because they booked without knowing what they were looking for.

Destination Photography · Kenya & Tanzania
East Africa
Kenya & Tanzania
The Masai Mara and Serengeti sit on opposite sides of an invisible border and form one continuous ecosystem — the greatest wildlife theater on earth. The Great Migration moves through both in a cycle that peaks in the Mara between July and October, when the wildebeest crossings produce the kind of wildlife drama that no amount of preparation makes ordinary. Kenya for the private conservancies around the Mara where no buses enter and guest numbers are capped. Tanzania for the Ngorongoro Crater, Selous, and the Serengeti's southern plains during calving season. A combined itinerary that covers both countries in ten to twelve days captures the full range of East African wildlife without redundancy.

Destination Photography · Botswana
Southern Africa
Botswana
The gold standard of exclusive safari travel. Botswana's low-volume, high-value tourism model limits the number of guests in each ecosystem and produces the most pristine wilderness experience available anywhere in Africa. The Okavango Delta — a UNESCO World Heritage Site where the Kalahari Desert floods to create a labyrinth of channels, islands, and lagoons — is genuinely unlike anything else on the continent. Chobe for the largest elephant herds in Africa. The Central Kalahari for solitude and lion behavior that the more-visited parks can't match. Best June through October in the dry season.

Destination Photography · Rwanda
Central Africa
Rwanda & Gorilla Country
Mountain gorilla trekking in the Volcanoes National Park is among the most affecting wildlife experiences in the world — one hour with a habituated gorilla family in the mist of a volcanic forest at altitude, close enough to look a silverback in the eye. Rwanda has rebuilt itself as one of Africa's most forward-thinking travel destinations, with lodges at the forest edge that sit among the finest small properties on the continent. Best year-round; June through September offers the driest conditions for the trek. Combine with Nyungwe Forest for chimpanzees and Akagera for savanna wildlife.

Southern Africa
Zambia & Victoria Falls
Victoria Falls is one of the genuine wonders of the world — the largest waterfall on earth by volume, best seen from the Zambian side between February and May when the flow is at maximum and the spray can be seen from twenty miles away. South Luangwa National Park for walking safaris with guides who are considered among the finest in Africa — the valley is where the walking safari tradition was invented, and the leopard density remains extraordinary. Lower Zambezi for canoeing past hippo pods and fishing tigers in the channels at dawn. A country for serious safari travelers who have done Kenya and Tanzania and want to go deeper.

North Africa
Morocco
The most culturally layered destination in Africa — a country where the medinas have been continuously inhabited for a thousand years and a guide who grew up in the Fez quarter is worth more than any amount of online research. The Fez medina with a scholar who understands the Islamic architecture and the medieval trading routes that built it. The Middle Atlas for Berber villages and cedar forests. The Sahara edge at Merzouga for a desert camp that earns the cliché. Marrakech for the souks, the riads, and a food scene that most visitors never find beneath the Djemaa el-Fna surface. What We Add to African Travel

Africa is the region where the planning work matters most. The gap between the right camp and the wrong one is wider here than anywhere else in the world.

⬡
Camp & Lodge Selection
There are over a thousand safari properties in sub-Saharan Africa. The list of camps that consistently deliver exceptional wildlife experiences and guide quality is short. We know it — built from firsthand visits, operator relationships, and client feedback across every major ecosystem. The difference between a camp on that list and one that isn't is frequently invisible in the brochure and immediately apparent on the ground.
⬡
Guide Quality
The best guide on a mediocre camp delivers a better safari than a mediocre guide on the best camp. We know which properties retain their top guides year over year, which camps have naturalists with specialist qualifications in specific ecosystems, and which operations have the guide-to-guest ratios that allow for the kind of personalized wildlife reading that makes a safari genuinely exceptional.
⬡
Timing & Migration Windows
The Great Migration is a continuous cycle — wildebeest in the Serengeti southern plains for calving in January and February, moving north through June and July, crossing into the Mara between July and October, returning south by November. Being in the right place in the right week requires understanding exactly where in that cycle the herds are. We track this annually and build itineraries around it rather than marketing a window and hoping the timing aligns.
⬡
Combination Itineraries
Africa's best trips are rarely single-country. Kenya and Tanzania together. South Africa's safari combined with the Cape. Rwanda's gorillas followed by the Mara. Zambia's walking safaris paired with Victoria Falls. We build these combination itineraries with the same rigor we apply to individual countries — ensuring that the logistics, the pacing, and the camp selection across multiple countries produce a coherent trip rather than a collection of disconnected experiences.
When to Go

Africa by Season

Great Migration Peak
July – October
The primary East Africa window — dry season in Kenya and Tanzania, peak wildlife viewing across the Mara and Serengeti, and the wildebeest river crossings at their most dramatic in July through September. Also the best window for Botswana (dry season concentrates animals at waterholes) and Zambia's South Luangwa. South Africa's Sabi Sands and Kruger perform excellently year-round but peak for leopard sightings in the dry winter months of June through August.
Best Overall Window
Green Season
November – March
The wet season in East and Southern Africa — lush landscapes, dramatically lower rates (30–50% at many camps), newborn wildlife in January and February, and the best bird-watching of the year. The Serengeti calving season from January through March is an extraordinary wildlife spectacle in its own right. Morocco and North Africa are ideal in winter — cool, dry, and uncrowded. Rwanda's gorilla trekking is excellent year-round. Not recommended for first-time safari travelers who want the maximum predator viewing.
Shoulder & Transition
April – June
April and May bring the long rains to East Africa — most camps close or operate at reduced capacity, and this window is generally not recommended for Kenya and Tanzania. However, the shoulder months of late April and May represent excellent value for South Africa, where the dry season is just beginning. June is the start of the peak window in Botswana and Zambia. Victoria Falls is at its most dramatic in March through May when the Zambezi is in full flood.

Common Questions

Africa — FAQ

Questions we hear most when planning Africa travel — from choosing the right country to timing, vaccinations, and combining destinations.

Which African country should a first-time safari traveler visit?+
South Africa is the most accessible first safari: strong infrastructure, no malaria in the Kruger private reserves, year-round big five viewing, and excellent luxury camps at a higher quality-to-cost ratio. If you're ready to go further, Kenya's Masai Mara during the Great Migration is one of the most extraordinary wildlife experiences in the world.
Is it safe to travel in Africa?+
Safety is destination and context-specific, not continental. The safari countries — South Africa, Kenya, Tanzania, Botswana, Rwanda — have well-established tourism infrastructure, professional operators, and dedicated wildlife areas with consistent security. I don't recommend specific regions within any country where the risk profile isn't appropriate for tourism.
Do I need vaccinations for Africa travel?+
It depends on the country and region. South Africa generally requires no vaccinations for most travelers. Kenya, Tanzania, and Botswana recommend or require malaria prophylaxis; yellow fever documentation may be required depending on transit countries. I provide a full health and entry brief with every itinerary and recommend consulting a travel health clinic 6–8 weeks before departure.
Can I combine multiple African countries in one trip?+
Yes, and many of the best itineraries do. Kenya and Tanzania are natural partners — the Masai Mara and Serengeti share the same ecosystem across a border. South Africa and Botswana are frequently combined, often adding Zimbabwe's Victoria Falls as a third country. Rwanda's gorilla trekking pairs well with a Kenya safari. These combinations add complexity but are very manageable with the right logistics.
What's the difference between East and Southern Africa for safari?+
East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania) is defined by the Great Migration, open savanna landscapes, and Maasai culture alongside the wildlife. Southern Africa (Botswana, Zimbabwe, South Africa, Zambia) offers deeper wilderness, more intimate camps with fewer vehicles at any sighting, and higher concentrations of certain species like wild dogs and large elephant herds.
When is the best time to travel to Africa?+
The dry season (May–October in most of Southern and East Africa) concentrates wildlife around water sources and keeps bush roads accessible. The Great Migration's dramatic river crossings peak July–October in Kenya and Tanzania. Gorilla trekking in Rwanda is possible year-round. The green season (November–April) offers lower prices, fewer visitors, and exceptional birdwatching.
What does it cost to do Africa properly?+
At the luxury end, camps in Botswana start around $700–$1,500 per person per night all-inclusive. A meaningful Africa itinerary of 8–12 days typically runs $10,000–$25,000+ per person depending on destination, timing, and camp selection. South Africa delivers a stronger quality-to-cost ratio. Budget expectations vary significantly by country and style of travel.
Is Africa appropriate for families with children?+
With the right property selection, yes. Many Botswana camps require a minimum age of 12; South Africa's private reserves are more family-accessible, with some camps welcoming children from age 6. Kenya has strong family-friendly options in the Masai Mara. I match families to properties that are genuinely set up for children — both in terms of programming and the daily rhythm of life in the bush.
