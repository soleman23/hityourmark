Why Cruises Matter

Not Every Ship
Is the Same Trip

Cruise travel gets painted with a broad brush — megaships, buffet lines, and ports that feel interchangeable. That version exists. So does a completely different one: a river ship docking in the center of a European town, a small expedition vessel in the Galápagos where every landing is timed around wildlife, a private yacht week in the Greek Islands where the itinerary flexes with the wind and your mood.

The difference isn't just price. It's knowing which lines actually deliver on their reputation, which cabin categories are worth the upgrade, which sailings sell out months ahead, and which routes look spectacular in a brochure but disappoint in shoulder season. Cabin position, dining programs, shore excursion quality, and embarkation logistics — these details compound.

We plan ocean cruises, river itineraries, small-ship expeditions, and private yacht charters. The right format depends on how you travel, who you're with, and what you want the days to feel like. That's the conversation we start with.

Cruise Season by Season

Peak Season
May – September · Dec – April
Mediterranean and European river peak runs late spring through early fall. Caribbean and yacht charter peak is December through April. Alaska is May through September. Book six to nine months out for the best cabin inventory on top sailings.

Shoulder Season
April – May · October – November
Strong value on Mediterranean and river itineraries — milder crowds, meaningful rate drops, and weather that is often still excellent. Caribbean shoulder edges work well for yacht charters if you accept a slightly higher chance of weather interruptions.

Common Questions

Cruises & Yachts — FAQ

Ocean vs. river vs. small-ship, cabin upgrades, and what actually separates a great sailing from a mediocre one.

What's the difference between ocean, river, and small-ship cruises?+
Ocean cruises cover long coastal and island itineraries on vessels ranging from mid-size luxury to megaships. River cruises dock in town centers, unpack once, and emphasize culture and scenery over sea days. Small-ship and expedition vessels (often under 200 guests) prioritize access — narrow fjords, wildlife landings, quieter anchorages. Private yacht charters put the itinerary entirely in your hands. The right format depends on how you want the days to feel.
Is cabin category really that important?+
Yes. On most ships, the gap between an interior cabin and a well-positioned balcony — or between a standard balcony and a suite — shapes the entire week. Midship cabins tend to be quieter and more stable; aft balconies often have more space and better views but more motion. I advise cabin by cabin, not by generic "upgrade if you can" advice.
When should I book a cruise?+
For popular Mediterranean, Alaska, and Caribbean peak sailings, six to nine months ahead is realistic if you want specific cabin categories. River cruises and yacht charters in peak season often book even earlier. Shoulder-season sailings and repositioning cruises can be planned closer in. Early booking also locks amenity programs and preferred partner benefits.
Are luxury cruise lines worth the premium?+
Often yes — when the line's actual product matches what you're paying for: better space ratios, inclusive dining and drinks, quieter decks, and stronger shore experiences. Not every "luxury" brand delivers equally. I recommend specific vessels and sailings based on track record, not marketing tiers alone.
What's involved in planning a private yacht charter?+
We match yacht size, crew quality, cuisine style, and region to your group — then handle the brokerage process, contracts, APA (advance provisioning allowance), and logistics around embarkation. Greek Islands, Croatia, the BVI, and French Polynesia are the routes we plan most. Peak weeks book far ahead; shoulder weeks can be more flexible.
Do you handle pre- and post-cruise hotels and flights?+
Yes. Embarkation cities reward an overnight before sailing — missed flights and same-day connections are the most common way to start a cruise badly. We arrange hotels, transfers, and flight timing so you board without stress, and we can extend the trip with land nights after disembarkation.
How do shore excursions work with an advisor?+
Ship-sponsored excursions are convenient but not always the best experience. For many ports, we arrange private guides or small-group alternatives that run on your schedule and avoid the megabus feel. On expedition sailings, landings are usually included and led by the ship's naturalists — different model entirely.
Is cruise travel right for first-timers?+
It can be — if the ship and itinerary match how you travel. First-timers often do well on mid-size ocean ships with strong itineraries, or on European river cruises where the logistics are simple. Megaships and highly structured entertainment-heavy sailings aren't for everyone. We start with how you want the days to feel, then recommend a format.
