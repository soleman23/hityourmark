Bangkok is one of the world's great food cities — not because of the street food alone, but because the entire spectrum from a three-baht morning market bowl to the multi-course tasting menu at a Nahm-generation restaurant operates simultaneously within a few kilometers of each other. The Country

Bangkok Is a Great Food City.
The North Is a Different Country.

Thailand's mistake — if it has one — is being so immediately accessible and so apparently similar across regions that the differences between the north and the south, between Bangkok and Chiang Mai, between Koh Lanta and Koh Tao, get flattened into a single "Thailand experience." They are not the same. Bangkok is a megacity with a food culture that spans from the Yaowarat Chinatown fisherman's dawn market to the modern Thai fine-dining movement that is among the most interesting in Asia. Chiang Mai operates at the pace of a market town; the hill tribe villages thirty minutes from the city center are a different world from either.

The Thai cooking tradition — Northern Thai in Chiang Mai, Central Thai in Bangkok, Southern Thai in the coastal regions — is not one cuisine. The fermented fish paste (pla ra) that defines Northern Thai cooking is absent from Bangkok's Central Thai repertoire; the coconut-heavy curries of the south have no equivalent in the north. A cooking class in Chiang Mai with a teacher who was trained by her grandmother in a Doi Suthep village teaches a different set of skills and ingredients than a Bangkok cooking class in a professional kitchen. Both are worth doing. Neither is interchangeable.

The island question has a right answer that depends entirely on what you want from it. Koh Lanta for families and snorkeling in calm water. Koh Tao for dive certification with the most affordable and well-organized dive schools in Thailand. Koh Samui for resort infrastructure and international flight access. Koh Lipe for the most beautiful beach water in Thailand with the least amount of development in front of it. Knowing which island is right for which traveler is planning work that makes the difference between the right beach trip and the wrong one.

Six Areas Worth Planning Properly

Thailand's regions are distinct enough that treating them as interchangeable is the most common planning error. Each has a specific best version that requires knowing what to look for.

Destination Photography · Bangkok
Central Thailand
Bangkok
The Damnoen Saduak floating market at dawn — not the tourist version where vendors are positioned for the camera, but the canal stalls where produce from the surrounding provinces is being traded wholesale before 7am, still operating on the same schedule it has kept for sixty years. Yaowarat Chinatown at 10pm when the barbecue smoke is thick and the street stalls run three-deep on the pavement from Odeon Circle to the river — a food guide who knows the hierarchy of Thai-Chinese cuisine maps this in an hour better than any guidebook in twenty pages. Wat Pho at 7am before the tour buses from the cruise ships arrive, the Reclining Buddha in the stillness of early morning rather than the noise of midday. Bangkok is one of the world's great cities and is systematically underexplored by travelers who allocate two nights between flights.

Destination Photography · Chiang Mai
Northern Thailand
Chiang Mai
The Doi Inthanon National Park hill tribe villages — Hmong and Karen settlements at 2,000 meters — are forty-five minutes from the city center and represent a cultural context unavailable anywhere else in mainland Southeast Asia. The Sunday Night Market on Wualai Road (the silversmith street) is a different experience from the tourist Walking Street on Rachadamnoen — the Sunday market still serves the city's own residents shopping for household goods and local food alongside the handicraft stalls. The Elephant Nature Park elephant sanctuary outside Chiang Mai is the genuine rehabilitation model; the camps using elephants for riding and performance are not. A cooking class with a teacher in her home village north of the city — not a commercial school — teaches Northern Thai cooking at the source.

Destination Photography · Chiang Rai & Golden Triangle
Far Northern Thailand
Chiang Rai & Golden Triangle
The White Temple — Wat Rong Khun — is the ongoing artistic project of Thai artist Chalermchai Kositpipat, a work of contemporary Buddhist architecture in white mirrored glass that has no precedent in Thai temple design and rewards spending more time than the standard tour bus stop allows. The Hall of Opium museum at the Golden Triangle is one of Southeast Asia's more honest historical museums, covering the economics of the colonial drug trade with a clarity that makes the Mekong border between Thailand, Laos, and Myanmar comprehensible in a new way. The Akha hill tribe coffee farmers near Mae Sai are growing some of the best coffee in Asia, in a supply chain that the specialty coffee industry is beginning to recognize.

Destination Photography · Southern Islands · Thailand
Southern Thailand
Southern Islands
Koh Lanta for families and couples who want calm water snorkeling, a low-key beach pace, and fewer party venues: the quietest large island in the Gulf of Thailand with a legitimate sea gypsy village on the north end. Koh Tao for PADI certification: the most affordable and best-organized dive schools in Thailand, in water with better visibility than the Andaman side in the Gulf months. Koh Samui for resort infrastructure, international flight access, and the luxury hotel options that are unavailable on the smaller islands. Koh Lipe for travelers who want the most beautiful beach water in Thailand without the development that defines Samui and Phangan — the island closest to the Malaysia border and furthest from the tourist infrastructure.

Destination Photography · Krabi & Railay
Andaman Coast
Krabi & Railay
Railay Beach — accessible only by longtail boat because the limestone karst cliffs that form its walls have no road entrance — is one of the most dramatic beach settings in Thailand: vertical cliff faces dropping to emerald water, sea kayaking through cave systems that open onto hidden lagoons accessible only at low tide. Phra Nang Cave Beach, at Railay's southern tip, is the best beach in Thailand by most measures and is most accessible before 9am when the longtails from Ao Nang begin arriving in volume. A private-charter 4 Islands boat trip covers the surrounding sea caves, snorkel sites, and the protected bay at Chicken Island in the half-day that the group tours rush through in four hours.

Destination Photography · Sukhothai
Lower Northern Thailand
Sukhothai & Ancient Temples
Sukhothai was the first capital of the Thai kingdom — a city established in the 13th century on a Buddhist cosmological plan, with temples arranged on a mandala that oriented the entire urban landscape around spiritual principles. The UNESCO World Heritage historical park that contains the ruins is best visited by bicycle at dawn, before the tour buses from Chiang Mai and Bangkok arrive, when the stone Buddha images emerge from the morning mist and the temple ponds reflect an early sky. A guide who understands the Buddhist architecture explains what the spatial relationships between the temples mean within the cosmology rather than simply reading the UNESCO date plaques. Sukhothai is the most underused major historical site in Thailand.

What We Add to Thailand Planning

Thailand planning errors are structural: wrong island for the traveler, wrong season for the region, north and south treated as the same destination. We correct all three.

⬡
Food Specialist Access
Bangkok is a great food city, and the guide who maps it by cuisine rather than by landmark is the one who unlocks it. The food specialists we work with in Bangkok know the Yaowarat hierarchy of Thai-Chinese cuisine, the morning market circuit that the professional chefs use for sourcing, and the omakase-level Thai restaurants that have a waiting list rather than a Tripadvisor page. In Chiang Mai, we work with teachers who are from the hill tribe communities that created Northern Thai cuisine — not instructors who learned it from a tourism curriculum.
⬡
Island Matching
The island question has a right answer for each traveler, and we ask the right questions before giving it. Koh Samui is right for travelers who want resort infrastructure and international flight convenience. Koh Lanta is right for families and snorkelers who want calm water and a quiet pace. Koh Tao is right for divers or dive students. Koh Lipe is right for travelers who want the finest beach without the development. Choosing incorrectly — most commonly selecting Koh Phangan for its beaches and discovering the Full Moon Party infrastructure, or choosing Koh Samui for the diving when Koh Tao is forty-five minutes away and significantly better — is the most common Thailand planning error we see.
⬡
Northern vs Southern Thailand
Most itineraries treat Thailand as Bangkok plus islands, which effectively ignores that northern Thailand — Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, the hill tribe country, Pai, Sukhothai — is an entirely different travel destination from the beach south. The food culture, the climate, the pace, the accommodation style, and the cultural context are all different. A Thailand itinerary that allocates five nights to the north and five nights to an island covers more of what the country actually is than a Bangkok transit plus ten-night beach trip, at the same total duration.
⬡
Avoiding Tourist Infrastructure
The difference between the Elephant Nature Park — a genuine rehabilitation sanctuary run by Lek Chailert since 1996 — and the elephant camps that offer riding and painting is not subtle and is not difficult to distinguish if you know what to look for. The difference between the floating market that operates for local commerce before 7am and the one that operates for tourist photography from 8am is the same thing. We build itineraries around the genuine version of each experience rather than the tourist-facing version that looks similar in the photograph and is not the same thing on the ground.
When to Go

Thailand by Season

Cool Dry Season
November – April
The correct answer for most travelers — the dry season runs throughout the country from November through April, with cool evenings in the north and clear skies in the south. Bangkok is most comfortable in November through February when temperatures stay below 32°C. Northern Thailand peaks in December and January with genuinely cool evenings and excellent visibility at altitude. The Andaman coast (Krabi, Phi Phi, Koh Lanta) is at its best from November through April. The Gulf coast islands (Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao) follow a slightly different pattern — they can have rain in November and December while the Andaman is clear. We provide specific guidance by island for every itinerary.
Best Overall Window
Shoulder & Festival Season
November & April
Loy Krathong in November — the festival of lights when lanterns are released on rivers across the country and the Chiang Mai sky fills with khom loi floating lanterns — is among the most visually spectacular cultural events in Southeast Asia. Songkran in mid-April (Thai New Year water festival) is equally spectacular and significantly wetter. Both festivals significantly affect accommodation availability and rates in the cities where they peak. Booking twelve months in advance for Loy Krathong in Chiang Mai is not unusual. April is also the hottest month in Thailand — Songkran provides a practical reason for the water.
Monsoon Season
May – October
The monsoon season runs May through October across most of Thailand, with rain concentrated in the afternoon and evening rather than all day. Bangkok is manageable with strategic indoor time during downpours. Northern Thailand is lush and photogenic in the rainy season and significantly quieter. The Andaman coast (Krabi, Koh Lanta, Phuket) is genuinely difficult in May through October — rough seas, limited boat access, and some islands closing entirely. The Gulf coast islands switch season — Koh Samui's drier period runs into November and December when the Andaman is clear, making the east coast the better island choice in September and October.

A Note from Mark

On Bangkok and the Food Question

Mark Sole
Founder & Travel Director · Hit Your Mark Travel

"Bangkok is one of the great food cities in the world. I say this in the same breath I would say it about Lyon, Tokyo, San Sebastian, or New York — not as hyperbole about a destination, but as a specific factual claim about the depth, range, and quality of what's available to eat within the city limits. Most visitors leave Bangkok having experienced perhaps 10% of it: the Pad Thai from the famous stall, the Som Tum from the street cart, the curry from the hotel restaurant. All of those things are real. None of them are the point. The point is the market at 5am, the vendor who has been making one dish for thirty years, the restaurant where the chef studied in France and came home to cook Thai food at a level the French tradition prepared her to reach. A food guide in Bangkok doesn't save you time. They give you access to something that is genuinely unavailable without them — and Bangkok is the city where that gap between available and inaccessible is the widest in Asia."

Common Questions

Thailand — FAQ

Questions we hear most when planning Thailand — from island selection and timing to elephant sanctuaries, solo female travel, and the Bangkok vs Chiang Mai home base question.

What is the best time to visit Thailand?+
November through April is the correct answer for most travelers — cool dry season throughout the country, clear skies on the Andaman coast, manageable temperatures in Bangkok and the north. December and January are the coolest months in Chiang Mai. The Gulf coast islands (Koh Samui, Koh Tao) operate on a slightly different schedule: September and October are actually drier on the Gulf side when the Andaman is in monsoon. If your priority is one specific island, the island's specific season matters more than the national calendar.
Which Thai island is right for me?+
Koh Samui for resort infrastructure, direct international flights, and the most developed luxury hotel sector on any Thai island. Koh Lanta for families and snorkelers wanting calm water and a quieter pace. Koh Tao for diving certification — the best organized and most affordable dive schools in Thailand. Koh Lipe for the finest beach water in Thailand with minimal development. Koh Phi Phi for the dramatic scenery if you can accept the party noise. Koh Phangan for Haad Rin beach — but not if you're going for the snorkeling; Koh Tao is forty-five minutes north and significantly better for that. The answer requires knowing what you want from the island.
How many nights should I spend in Bangkok before heading north or south?+
Three nights is the minimum that does Bangkok justice — enough for the temple circuit at dawn, the food guide market and night walk, and a meaningful experience of the river and the neighborhoods rather than the hotel lobby and the Skytrain. Two nights is transit; it produces the sense of having visited without the substance of having been there. For travelers who want Bangkok to be a genuine part of the trip rather than an arrival pad, three nights is our consistent recommendation. Four nights is appropriate if Bangkok's food culture is itself a primary interest.
What is the real difference between Koh Samui and Phuket?+
Koh Samui is a Gulf coast island with its own international airport, developed resort infrastructure, and a calmer sea on the northern and eastern coasts. Phuket is an Andaman coast island — larger, with more developed infrastructure and more tourist density than Samui, and with the Andaman's characteristically clearer water and stronger swell. The best beach areas of Phuket (Surin, Kamala, the north) are significantly better than the overbuilt Patong area that most visitors encounter. Koh Samui suits families and travelers who want resort calm; Phuket suits travelers who want more activity options and are willing to navigate to the less developed beaches. Neither is the answer for serious snorkelers or divers — Koh Lanta or Koh Tao does that better.
How do I distinguish a genuine elephant sanctuary from a commercial camp?+
The clearest indicator: does the operation use elephants for riding, painting, or performance? If yes, it is not a sanctuary — those activities require training methods that no ethical rehabilitation operation uses. The Elephant Nature Park in Chiang Mai, founded by Lek Chailert, is the most established genuine sanctuary in northern Thailand — elephants are not ridden, are not trained to perform, and the human-elephant interaction involves bathing, feeding, and walking alongside rather than on top of. Several newer operations have adopted the sanctuary vocabulary while maintaining the training model in less visible ways. We specify the properties we recommend by name, not by category.
Is Thailand safe for solo female travelers?+
Thailand is consistently rated among the most solo-female-friendly destinations in Southeast Asia — Bangkok, Chiang Mai, and the major islands have well-established infrastructure for solo travelers and strong backpacker and independent travel networks. Standard urban safety precautions apply in Bangkok (use Grab rather than unmetered taxis, avoid poorly lit areas at night). The island resorts are generally very safe. Temple dress code (knees and shoulders covered for entry) is the most commonly encountered cultural consideration. We provide specific neighborhood and transport guidance for solo travelers as part of every Thailand itinerary.
Is Thailand good for vegetarians?+
Excellent — the Buddhist vegetarian tradition in Thailand produces a restaurant category (the "jay" or vegan Thai kitchen, marked by a yellow flag) that has no equivalent in most Western countries: inexpensive, widely available, and genuinely skilled in producing the full range of Thai flavors without animal products. Bangkok's Chinatown and the Buddhist temple neighborhoods have the highest concentration of jay restaurants. Beyond dedicated vegetarian restaurants, Thai cuisine includes a large number of inherently vegetarian dishes in its standard repertoire (many curries, most rice dishes, stir-fried vegetables). Fish sauce is the hidden challenge — it appears in many dishes without being listed as an ingredient, and requesting "no fish sauce" requires specific communication rather than ordering vegetarian.
Is Bangkok or Chiang Mai a better home base for a Thailand trip?+
Neither is better as a standalone base — they are complementary destinations that serve different purposes. Bangkok is the international gateway and the food and culture capital; Chiang Mai is the northern gateway to the hill tribes, the cooking tradition, and the mountain landscape. For a first Thailand trip of ten or more days, both deserve at least three nights each. For shorter trips where a single base is required, Bangkok wins for international connectivity and the widest range of daily activity; Chiang Mai wins for travelers whose priority is the northern food and cultural traditions rather than the city-scale Bangkok offers.
