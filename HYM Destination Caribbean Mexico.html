Caribbean & Mexico

The Right Island
Changes Everything.

The Caribbean and Mexico contain some of the finest beach and resort experiences in the world — and some of the most disappointing. The islands look similar on a map and in photographs. On the ground, the differences between them are the whole trip. Getting this right starts before the booking.

The Region

Not All Sand and Blue
Water Is the Same

Turks & Caicos and the Dominican Republic are both Caribbean. The water in both is warm. The sand in both is white. The experience in both is categorically different — and the price difference between the two is not always what travelers assume it will be. St. Barth's is expensive because it is genuinely exclusive and genuinely exceptional. Jamaica at the right property in the right parish is one of the most culturally rich experiences in the region. Los Cabos in Mexico is a desert peninsula meeting the Pacific where the right villa community and the right time of year produces something that no all-inclusive can approach.

The planning work in this region is more consequential than almost anywhere else because the stakes are higher and the surface-level research is more misleading. Photos of turquoise water and white sand look identical across a dozen islands with very different realities. The right question is never which island — it's which property, which room category, which month, and which version of the destination you're actually looking for.

We've been planning Caribbean and Mexico travel long enough to know the properties that deliver, the islands that have earned their reputation, and the months that make or break a trip. The research doesn't start at the destination — it starts with understanding exactly what kind of trip you're trying to have.

Destination Photography · Turks & Caicos
British West Indies
Turks & Caicos
Grace Bay Beach is consistently ranked among the finest beaches in the world — and the ranking is accurate. Twelve miles of uninterrupted white sand and water that is genuinely that color in person. The island is small enough to navigate without a car, exclusive enough to feel uncrowded even at peak season, and close enough to the eastern seaboard to feel like an escape that doesn't require a full week. The right villa properties on the north shore give families and groups the space and privacy that resort rooms cannot. Best December through April. Worth booking six months out for the top properties.

Destination Photography · St. Barth's
French West Indies
St. Barth's
The most genuinely exclusive island in the Caribbean — small, French, with an airport approach that requires a pilot who has done it before and a clientele that comes back every year because the island earns it. Villa stays are the correct way to experience it: a private pool, a provisioned kitchen, a hillside terrace overlooking Gustavia. The restaurant scene punches well above the island's size. Best December through March at the peak of the season; significantly quieter — and less expensive — in late April and May.

Destination Photography · Jamaica
Greater Antilles
Jamaica
The most culturally specific island in the Caribbean — a place with its own music, its own food tradition, its own way of conducting time. The right property in the right parish makes Jamaica exceptional: boutique all-inclusives on the cliffs above the sea in the west, small-ship excursions to the Blue Mountains in the east, a jerk chicken lunch at a roadside spot that the hotel guide knows by name. Avoid the all-inclusive mega-resorts near Montego Bay if culture is part of what you're looking for.

Barbados & Eastern Caribbean
Barbados for the west coast — the Platinum Coast has a handful of properties that have been doing luxury correctly for decades, a food scene that outperforms its size, and a British-Caribbean cultural overlay that makes it feel genuinely different from the rest of the region. St. Lucia for the Pitons and the volcanic mountain landscape that frames a beach resort in a way no flat island can replicate. Destination Photography · Mexico
Mexico
Riviera Maya & Los Cabos
Two very different versions of Mexico for two very different trips. Riviera Maya for the cenotes, the Tulum ruins, the adults-only boutique properties in the jungle that bear no resemblance to the hotel zone. Los Cabos for the desert peninsula where the Pacific meets the Sea of Cortez — private villa communities, deep-sea fishing at dawn, a restaurant scene that has matured significantly over the past decade. Both reward the same approach: ignore the all-inclusive category and book the property that earns the room rate.

Destination Photography · Dominican Republic
Greater Antilles
Dominican Republic
The most value-dense destination in the Caribbean when planned correctly — not the mass-market hotel zone near Punta Cana, but the boutique all-inclusives in Las Terrenas on the Samaná Peninsula, the golf resorts in Casa de Campo, and the underexplored north coast near Cabarete where the kite-surfing is some of the finest in the world. The DR is where the all-inclusive format actually works at its best — at the right properties, it's a strong answer to the question of where to take a family on a budget that doesn't want to feel like one.

What We Add to Caribbean & Mexico Travel

The Caribbean and Mexico look simple to book. The detail work that separates a good trip from a forgettable one is less obvious here than almost anywhere — and more consequential when it goes wrong.

The difference between a garden-view room and a beachfront suite at the same property is frequently the difference between a good trip and an exceptional one. We don't book the room that fits the budget without making sure it fits the trip.

⬡
Seasonal & Weather Guidance
Hurricane season in the Caribbean runs June through November, with the highest risk in September and October. Most travelers know this and still book into the window thinking odds are on their side. We advise on the actual risk profile by island, the insurance options worth carrying, and the windows that deliver the same pricing without the exposure — most of which fall in May, early June, and late November.
When to Go

Caribbean & Mexico by Season

Peak Season
December – April
The primary Caribbean and Mexico window — dry, calm, and consistently excellent across every destination in the region. Christmas and New Year's are the most expensive two weeks of the year; January through March offers the same weather at meaningfully lower rates. Top properties at Turks & Caicos and St. Barth's book out six months in advance for February. Book early.
Best Overall Window
Summer
June – August
The Caribbean in summer is warmer, more humid, and cheaper — meaningfully so. The water remains excellent. School-holiday timing makes June and July the primary family window despite the heat. Los Cabos in summer is one of the best value propositions in the region — the Pacific side stays cooler than the Caribbean, hotel rates drop significantly, and the fishing season peaks for marlin and dorado. Hurricane risk is present but typically low before September.
Shoulder & Off-Season
May & November
The two best-kept secrets in Caribbean travel. May is post-peak and pre-hurricane — rates are 30–40% lower than February, weather is excellent, and the islands are noticeably quieter. Late November after Thanksgiving is similar: the peak-season machine is warming up, but rates haven't caught up yet. September and October carry genuine hurricane risk and are not recommended for travel to the Caribbean without comprehensive travel insurance and flexible dates.

Common Questions

Caribbean & Mexico — FAQ

Questions about island selection, hurricane season, resort choices, and how to build a trip that goes beyond the standard package.

What's the difference between Caribbean islands?+
Significant. St. Barts is small, private, and Parisian in character — expensive, no chain hotels, excellent food. Turks and Caicos is about beaches and water clarity — some of the clearest in the world — with limited cultural activity. Jamaica has music, food culture, and character alongside strong resort options. St. Lucia is scenically dramatic. Barbados blends English heritage with a sophisticated dining scene. The right island depends entirely on what you want the trip to feel like.
When is hurricane season in the Caribbean?+
Officially June 1 through November 30, with the highest-risk period typically August–October. Many travelers book July–November at lower rates, accepting a small but real probability of disruption. Islands south of the traditional hurricane belt — Aruba, Bonaire, Curaçao, Trinidad — carry meaningfully lower risk. Travel insurance during this window is strongly advisable.
What are the best luxury resort options in Mexico?+
Los Cabos has the deepest inventory of luxury hotels at the tip of Baja California — the Corridor has produced some of Mexico's most design-forward properties. Riviera Maya (Tulum through Playa del Carmen) offers both ultra-luxury boutique options and family-friendly all-inclusive resorts. Puerto Vallarta and Punta Mita deliver a more authentically Mexican character with world-class resort options.
Can I combine multiple Caribbean islands in one trip?+
Yes — inter-island flights make this manageable. Popular combinations include Turks and Caicos with the Bahamas, Barbados with St. Lucia, or a broader Eastern Caribbean island hop. Adding Havana or San Juan as a city anchor works well. I design multi-island itineraries around how each destination complements the others in pace, character, and activity.
Is the Riviera Maya or Los Cabos better for a first Mexico trip?+
Different experiences. The Riviera Maya has proximity to Mayan ruins (Chichen Itza, Tulum, Cobá), cenotes, and deeper cultural context alongside its resorts. Los Cabos is more resort-focused, with dramatic desert-meets-ocean scenery, excellent sport fishing, and some of Mexico's finest luxury properties. The right choice depends on whether you want cultural depth or pure resort experience.
How do I choose between all-inclusive and independent resorts in the Caribbean?+
The all-inclusive model removes billing friction and works well when you plan to stay on-property most of the time. Independent resorts give more flexibility and often deliver better food and more individualized service. At the top end, the distinction narrows significantly. I advise based on your travel style and what you actually want from the trip, not a blanket preference for one model.
What's the best island for a honeymoon in the Caribbean?+
St. Barts, Turks and Caicos, and St. Lucia are the three I recommend most often for honeymoons. St. Barts for privacy, sophistication, and food. Turks for the beach and water quality. St. Lucia for dramatic scenery, a sense of place, and a combination of activity and relaxation. The right answer depends on your budget and what you value most in a honeymoon experience.
Do I need a passport to visit the Caribbean or Mexico?+
US citizens traveling to Mexico and most Caribbean destinations require a valid US passport for reentry — a passport card works for some land and sea crossings but not air travel. US territories (Puerto Rico, USVI) don't require a passport for US citizens. Entry requirements for non-US travelers vary by destination. I verify requirements for every client and flag any documentation that needs advance preparation.
